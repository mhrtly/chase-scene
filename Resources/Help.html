Chase Scene 🎷

Chase music whenever an AI drives your Mac.

What it does

When another program starts moving or clicking your mouse (which is how computer-use AIs drive a Mac), Chase Scene plays chase music. It keeps playing through the AI's thinking pauses, then fades out about 20 seconds after the last move.

Click the ♪ in your menu bar to mute the music, change the volume, preview it, or open Settings. While an AI is driving, the icon turns into a green runner. An orange ? means a connected AI tool stopped reporting in the middle of a session. The music keeps going until the session is explicitly released, so silence never lies to you.

“That wasn't an AI!”

Some ordinary utilities also move the pointer in software: mouse-button remappers, window managers, remote-desktop tools. While one is playing music, open the menu and choose Not an AI? Ignore “…”. You can undo it under Settings → Ignored apps. To turn automatic detection off completely, use Settings → Detect AI mouse control automatically.

Your own song

The built-in tune, Hot Potato Hustle, is an original composition made for this app. To use a song of your own, choose Settings → Choose your own song… and pick any audio file you have (MP3, M4A, WAV, AIFF).

Exact timing for Claude Code and Codex (optional)

Choose Settings → Connect Claude Code… or Connect Codex…. The music then starts the moment a computer-use tool is called, before the first click, and stops when the AI's turn ends. Chase Scene adds only its own hook entries, keeps everything else in the file, and saves a backup first. Disconnect from the same menu.

  • Claude Code: start a new session afterwards. Hooks live in ~/.claude/settings.json.
  • Codex: run /hooks in Codex and approve the Chase Scene entries, then start a new session. Hooks live in ~/.codex/hooks.json.

Once a connected tool sends its first signal, its line in Settings shows working ✓.

Any other AI tool (MCP)

Choose Settings → Copy MCP server config and paste it into your tool's MCP settings. The tool gets begin_control, keepalive, end_control and control_status.

From scripts

chase="$HOME/Library/Application Support/Chase Scene/bin/chase-scene"
"$chase" signal '{"action":"begin","session_id":"my-run","agent":"My Agent"}'
"$chase" signal '{"action":"end","session_id":"my-run"}'
"$chase" status

Privacy

Everything stays on this Mac: there's no network access, no telemetry and no screen recording, and Chase Scene asks for no special permissions. To tell your hand from software, it reads one field of each mouse event, the ID of the program that posted it, and records nothing. Hook integrations look only at tool names. Chase Scene is an indicator, not a lock. It never blocks input and can't stop an AI. Silence can also mean the music is muted, the app is closed, or an AI is working without moving the pointer.
